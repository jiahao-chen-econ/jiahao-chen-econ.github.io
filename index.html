JOB MARKET PAPER

Innovation Policy in Product-Line Markets: Evidence from GPUs

What does society gain from R&D, and what can policy add?

Paper (latest PDF) ↗
Abstract

In this paper, I use a dynamic oligopoly model with endogenous pricing, product-line adjustment, and R&D to examine the effects of innovation policies in product-line markets. I apply the model to the desktop graphics processing unit (GPU) market, a duopoly between Nvidia and AMD. I find that a dollar of R&D generates about $9 of within-market surplus net of that dollar, with the firms capturing $3 and consumers the remainder. However, the return to public support is far more modest. I simulate three stylized interventions: an R&D credit, and two product rebates, one at the cutting edge and one on the mass market. Only the R&D credit induces a material increase in private R&D, but it returns only $0.51 of surplus per public dollar because additionality is low: most of its outlay reimburses research the firms would have conducted anyway. The rebate on cutting-edge products returns only $0.23 per public dollar and sets back the technology frontier. By subsidizing both firms, the rebate ignites a price war at the top of the line, benefiting consumers but undercutting the reason firms advance the frontier. The mass-market rebate sets off the same war lower down the product line but expands adoption enough to sustain R&D and returns $1.09 per public dollar. These results suggest that innovation policy in product-line markets is a more intricate design problem than in markets with simpler structures. Each instrument induces a distinct equilibrium response in prices, qualities, and R&D. The static and dynamic components of that response can offset or reinforce each other, attenuating or even reversing the intended effect.
